Read the currency before the number.

Every figure on this page is somebody else’s data. No benchmark vendor publishes Australian CPMs in Australian dollars — Superads, Triple Whale and Lebesgue all report in US dollars from US platforms. Australian pages that relabel those figures as AUD overstate them by about 40%.

So USD is the figure of record here. Where an AUD number appears it is a conversion at USD 1 = AUD 1.40 for orientation, not a measured Australian benchmark. Data window is July 2025 to July 2026 unless a row says otherwise.

Australian all-industry median: about USD $9.60 to $21.70 a month (Superads).
Australia runs roughly 28–30% below the United States on two independent datasets.
It is still one of the most expensive markets in the world, alongside the UK and Canada.
There is no Australian industry-level CPM dataset in the public domain. The industry rows below are global.

Why your CPM will not match this.

Industry predicts CPM less well than benchmark pages imply. Campaign objective moves it more — a purchase campaign and a reach campaign in the same industry can differ by more than the gap between two industries — and almost no published benchmark splits by objective.

Placement mix matters too. Reels and Stories monetise below feed, so a Reels-heavy account reports a lower blended CPM on identical targeting. Creative quality is priced directly: Meta charges less per thousand for ads it predicts will earn attention, and that is invisible in a median.

Broad audiences deliver cheaper impressions than tight ones. Retargeting costs more and converts better.
November is the peak. Cyber Monday 2024 ran 138% above the annual average in US data.
Late December to mid January is the cheapest inventory of the year.
iOS did not raise CPMs. It raised measured CPA by hiding conversions.
A 5% move in AUD/USD shifts your reported CPM by 5% with no change in the auction.
Meta’s average price per ad rose 12% year on year in Q1 and Q2 2026. Any benchmark drifts.

Questions people actually ask.

What is a good CPM for Facebook ads in Australia?

Between July 2025 and July 2026 the Australian all-industry monthly median ran from about USD $9.60 to $21.70, averaging around USD $14.40. Anything inside that band is normal. Whether it is good depends on what those impressions convert to.

Are Facebook ads cheaper in Australia than the US?

Yes, by roughly 28 to 30% on CPM, on two independent datasets. Australia is still one of the most expensive markets in the world, in the same tier as the UK and Canada.

Why are these benchmarks in US dollars?

Because that is how every vendor with a disclosed methodology publishes them. Nobody publishes an Australian industry benchmark in AUD from real ad account data, so we convert at a stated rate rather than relabel the figures.

Which industries have the highest CPMs?

Finance, real estate, and health and wellness. Finance and real estate also swing the widest, above USD $40 in some months and below $11 in others, which points at small samples as much as genuine cost.

Which industries are cheapest?

Automotive parts ecommerce at about USD $7, fashion at USD $8 to $13, and travel at USD $9 to $16.

How much do CPMs rise at Black Friday?

Cyber Monday 2024 was 138% above the annual average in US agency data, and Black Friday week about 80% above. Australian monthly medians peaked in November 2025 at about 51% above the annual average. Across the whole quarter the premium is smaller, historically 13 to 17%. Budget for the week, not the quarter.

Is there Australian data by industry?

No. We looked. Home services and trades, hospitality, automotive dealers, and banking split from insurance have no aggregated ad account data with a stated sample, period and currency, so they are not in the table. In those categories your own account history is a better benchmark than anything public.

How often is this updated?

The underlying sources refresh monthly or annually. This page is reviewed quarterly against them.
