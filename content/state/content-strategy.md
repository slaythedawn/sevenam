# Content strategy: the series, the modes, the rhythm

Written 29 Aug 2026 on Josh's instruction: develop content themes that repeat on a
schedule, across four modes. Read with `audience.md`, which says who this is for and where
they are going.

**The principle.** A feed works when a viewer starts to recognise what they are looking at
before they read it. That needs named series that recur, each with its own visual treatment,
not a stream of one-off posts. Eight series, four modes, one palette.

---

## The four modes, and the weekly split

Josh's framing, 29 Aug 2026.

| Mode | What it does | Share of the week |
|---|---|---|
| **Aspiration** | Sells the position they end up holding | ~2 in 7 |
| **Informative** | Proof from inside real accounts | ~3 in 7 |
| **Education** | The method, given away in full | ~2 in 7 |
| **Lifestyle** | Training and ordinary life | ~2 in 7 |

Overlaps because Instagram carries more lifestyle and LinkedIn more education. The mix
matters more than the arithmetic.

## The eight series

### 1. 7am — *informative*

The daily decision, written before anything else. The signature ritual and the only thing
here nobody else can post.

**Treatment.** Phone-filmed screen at dawn, volt redaction bars over every figure.
**Cadence.** Daily when the numbers exist. **Never invent one to fill the slot.**
**Channels.** All three.

### 2. Inside the Account — *informative*

The format the reference posts use, rebuilt so it is publishable. A phone filming a real
screen: the queue of creative, the account structure, a decision being made, the volume of
work going out.

**The rule that makes it possible.** Film the system, redact the money. **Volt bars over
every spend figure, ROAS, revenue number and client name.** No exceptions, no "it is only a
small number". The redaction is the design, and it says real account, numbers withheld,
which is both true and more interesting than the figure would have been.

**Treatment.** Filmed off the screen on a phone, never a clean screenshot. Slight angle,
moiré, a hand or finger in frame. Plain text overlay, black on white, two lines maximum.
**Cadence.** Two a week. **Channels.** Instagram and LinkedIn; a still to X.

### 3. The Teardown — *informative and education*

A public ad from the Meta Ad Library, dismantled. Free, infinite, unfakeable, and it touches
no client account at all.

**Treatment.** The ad, marked up with the volt annotation layer — circles, arrows, a rule
under the thing that matters. **Cadence.** One a week. **Channels.** LinkedIn and Instagram
carousel.

### 3b. The Build — *aspiration and informative, and the most important one here*

**Added 29 Aug 2026 after Josh pointed out it was missing.** It should have been first. Both
style references he gave are this format and nothing else:

- **Nacho Average Application.** He did not send a CV for the GYG marketing role. He made a
  seven video campaign instead. **One hour. Fifty dollars.** Written up with the method.
- **The Isabella film.** A finished cinematic campaign shared, then the reveal that the model
  never existed and all of it was generated.

**The shape: make something, absurdly fast and cheap, and show the receipt.** The time and
the cost are the whole payload, because they are the proof of the thesis. Nobody argues with
a thing that exists.

This is the format that carries the entire positioning without ever stating it. "I build
products and grow businesses at speed" is an assertion. Seven videos in an hour for fifty
dollars is evidence, and the reader does the arithmetic themselves. It also serves both
audiences at once: a founder sees what is now possible, a marketer sees something they can
take into a meeting.

**Treatment.** The work itself is the post. Frames from the thing, or the thing in full.
**Cadence.** One a fortnight, ideally. It is the highest-effort series and the highest value.
**Channels.** All three, natively per the routing rules.

**The rule.** Always publish the method and the numbers that are *his own* — time, cost,
tools, how it was made. Those are not client figures and no guardrail touches them. That is
what makes it a Build rather than a showreel.

### 3c. In the Room — *lifestyle and informative*

Events, rooms, people, the industry in person. The Tomi Grainger event post that was drafted
and never went out is the template, and its failure is the lesson: **it needed a tag, tagging
cannot be automated, so it sat at POST BY HAND and died.**

**Treatment.** Real photographs, unstyled. Instagram carousel for a set, LinkedIn takes the
single strongest frame. **Cadence.** Whenever it actually happens, never manufactured.
**Channels.** All three. **Plan it as manual from the start** when it names anyone.

### 3d. The Tool — *education*

A thing the reader can use, published as a post. The `/agency-fee` calculator is one and it
is currently the only one. A calculator, a checklist, a template, a spreadsheet.

**Treatment.** Screen recording of it being used, or a card carrying the method with the tool
in the first comment. **Cadence.** Rare, because they have to be built.
**Channels.** LinkedIn and Instagram, link in first comment.

### 4. The Method — *education*

How the work is actually done, published in full. No withheld step, no gated framework.

**Treatment.** Design-led. Cards, a framework, a diagram. This is where the four design-led
posts a week live. **Cadence.** One a week. **Channels.** LinkedIn, Instagram carousel.

### 5. One Thing — *education*

A single mechanism, explained short. Why a metric lies, what frequency is telling you, what
leaves the learning phase.

**Treatment.** Statement card or a marked-up frame. **Cadence.** Two or three a week.
**Channels.** X primarily, it is the format X rewards.

### 6. The Position — *aspiration*

Where they end up. An account they own, margin that survives their own growth, a business
somebody else could run or buy. This is the aspiration mode and it is aimed at **their**
destination, never at what Josh owns.

**Treatment.** The aspirational photography — scale, standard, early light, real places.
Sydney is genuinely aspirational and genuinely available.
**Cadence.** Two a week. **Channels.** All three.

### 7. The Mission — *lifestyle*

Training. Black and white, obscured. Personal growth beside the work, never explained as a
metaphor for it.

**Treatment.** High contrast monochrome, **no volt at all**, so the grid separates the two
threads at a glance. **Cadence.** One or two a week, never consecutive days.
**Channels.** Instagram led.

### 8. Off Duty — *lifestyle*

Ordinary life. Family, the car, the walk, the coffee, the thing that went wrong. Warmth and
proof of a real person.

**Treatment.** Real photographs, unstyled. **Cadence.** One a week. **Channels.** Instagram,
occasionally LinkedIn.

## Not a roster. A palette.

**Corrected 29 Aug 2026, an hour after the first version.** Josh: *"Social media should be
messy, not so predictable."* He is right, and the first draft of this file was a timetable —
Monday is always The Position, Tuesday is always Inside the Account. That is a content
calendar, and it reads like one from the outside.

**What recurs is the treatment, not the timing.** Someone who has seen three Teardowns knows
what a marked-up frame means before they read a word. That recognition is the whole benefit
of having series, and it survives completely without a schedule. Nothing is gained by
Tuesday always being the same shape, and something real is lost: an account that posts the
same shape every Tuesday was obviously planned by somebody, and it reads that way.

**So the eight series are a palette to draw from, never a rota to work through.**

### The mess rules

1. **Draw by what is true today, not by whose turn it is.** If the account did something
   interesting this morning, that is the post. The plan is a list of options, not a list of
   slots.
2. **Let reality interrupt.** Anything that actually happened outranks anything planned.
   Bin the plan without ceremony.
3. **Run in bursts.** Three posts on one idea across two days, then leave that thread alone
   for a fortnight. Real interest is lumpy. Even distribution is the tell.
4. **Vary the finish, hard.** Roughly one post in four should look like it took four
   minutes, because it did. A phone photo, one line, no card, no crop. A feed where every
   post is produced to the same standard looks like a campaign.
5. **Vary length past the point of comfort.** Nine words next to three hundred. The
   published length mix is a floor, not a rhythm.
6. **Do not centre the window.** The timing windows exist for reach, but posting at the same
   point inside them every day is the same signature as a fixed minute.
7. **Leave things unresolved.** A thought that goes nowhere. An observation with no lesson
   attached. Not everything needs a landing.
8. **React, do not only publish.** Quote posts, replies, a screenshot of something someone
   else said. Cheap, fast, and the most human thing on the account.
9. **Break a series on purpose** every few weeks. A post that fits none of the eight.

### Two devices worth stealing, both from references Josh sent

**The recurring line.** Davie Fogarty burns the same phrase onto every frame, every post.
"Vision first, everything follows." It is why his posts are recognisable in half a second,
and it costs nothing. Sevenam should have one line that appears on the work, in the same
place, in the same type, indefinitely. **Not a tagline written for an ad** — a sentence he
would actually say, that gets repeated until it belongs to him. Do not invent one in a
hurry; it has to be right, because the value is entirely in the repetition.

**Frames from one continuous video.** Josh's own instruction, 28 Aug: shoot one unbroken
phone take walking through the actual thing, then cut it into stills with one bold line on
each. It carries a narrative across frames, it is obviously real because it is one take, and
the same footage yields a Reel, a Story sequence and a carousel. Highest effort-to-payoff
format available, and it needs no generated imagery at all.

### The thing that stays disciplined

**Mess in what and when. Discipline in how it looks.** The volt treatments, the palette, the
claim rules and the approval gate never loosen. An unpredictable account with a consistent
hand reads as a person. An unpredictable account with an inconsistent hand reads as
abandoned.

### The remaining predictable element, flagged not argued

The no-skip-a-day rule is itself a metronome: one post per channel per day, forever, is a
pattern visible from orbit. Josh overrode the old vary-the-cadence rule deliberately on
28 Aug and that stands. Worth knowing that it is now the largest regular signal in the
system, and that the mess has to come from format, length, finish and order instead.

### What a real fortnight looks like

Uneven on purpose. Series recur without a pattern, some days carry two posts on a channel,
one day gets hijacked, one post is deliberately throwaway.

| Day | Roughly |
|---|---|
| 1 | Teardown, long. X gets two, hours apart |
| 2 | Off Duty, one line and a bad photo. Nothing else |
| 3 | Inside the Account, filmed screen. Then a follow-up that afternoon because a reply asked |
| 4 | The Method, the week's produced piece |
| 5 | Quiet. One short One Thing on each channel |
| 6 | The Mission |
| 7 | 7am, because the numbers were interesting |
| 8 | Nothing planned survives: something happened, post that |
| 9 | The Position |
| 10 | Second Teardown in ten days, because the first one landed |
| 11 | Off Duty. A throwaway |
| 12 | Inside the Account, then One Thing four hours later |
| 13 | The Method as a carousel |
| 14 | A post that fits none of the series |

## Video, and why it is now in the mix

The reference material Josh flagged is video, and the strongest of it is barely produced: a
phone pointed at a screen while a finger moves down a column. That is inside the reach of
this system today.

**Two video formats to build first.**

**The screen sequence.** Four to six frames filmed off one continuous take, one line of text
each, each advancing the argument. Works as a Reel, a Story sequence and a carousel from the
same footage.

**The napkin.** A mechanism drawn by hand and photographed or filmed. Low fidelity is the
point — it cannot be faked and it reads as thinking rather than marketing.

Reels are produced through Higgsfield, hosted under `social/` on the site, then published.
See the skill for the pipeline.

## The treatments, which are what hold it together

| Treatment | Used by | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Volt mark-up** | Teardown, One Thing | B&W photo, precise volt annotation applied in code |
| **Volt redaction** | 7am, Inside the Account | Filmed screen, volt bars over every figure |
| **Design-led card** | The Method | Ink card, volt figure, full bleed |
| **Documentary B&W** | The Mission | High contrast monochrome, no volt |
| **Aspirational photograph** | The Position | Scale and standard, real places |
| **Real photograph** | Off Duty | Unstyled, phone, ordinary |

**Monochrome plus volt, everywhere except The Mission**, which drops volt so the two threads
read apart. That single constraint is what makes eight series look like one account.

## What this does not change

The approval gate. The claim rules. No invented number, ever, and no client identifiable.
The reference posts that inspired the Inside the Account format publish live ROAS and spend
freely. **Sevenam cannot and the redaction is not a compromise, it is the format.**
