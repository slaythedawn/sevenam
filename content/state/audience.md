# The audience, and where they are trying to get to

Researched and written 29 Aug 2026, after Josh's correction that the content is about the
reader's destination rather than about him or the offer. This file is the answer to "who is
this for and what do they want", and every post is checked against it.

Sources are listed at the end. Market figures here are **external research about the market**,
not Sevenam claims, and the distinction matters for guardrail 2.

---

## Who they are

**Australian ecommerce founders and brand owners spending $30k to $500k a month on Meta.**
Published on /index and /about, so this is the stated market, not an assumption.

They are operators. They carry the P&L, they sign the invoices, and they are the person who
decides whether an agency stays. They are not marketers, they are not looking for peers, and
they are not reading for commentary on the platform.

## Where they are trying to get to

The single most useful thing the research surfaced: **the goal has moved.** The 2020 to 2022
era of buying growth at any margin is over. In 2026 the thing founders are building toward is
a business that is **sellable**, and the market pays for a specific shape:

| What the market pays for, 2026 | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Profitability and unit economics that already work** | Top of every buyer's evaluation framework |
| **EBITDA margin of 10 to 15% minimum** | For brands doing $5m to $50m |
| **Multiples of 3.0x to 6.0x EBITDA** | Mid-sized private. Scaled and diversified reach 6.0x to 10.0x+ |
| **Smaller brands: 1.5x to 3.5x SDE** | Under about $5m |

And the gap that makes it interesting: **the median eight-figure DTC brand runs an EBITDA
margin of about 7 to 8%**, below the 10% floor most buyers want. Brands that clear it attract
outsized attention.

**Preparation takes 18 to 24 months** and rushing it reportedly costs 30 to 50% of the
valuation. So the work is done long before anyone talks to a broker.

### Why that is the destination for this content, and not a detour

A percentage-of-spend agency fee is not a marketing cost to a buyer. **It is an EBITDA line
item that grows with success**, and EBITDA is the number the multiple is applied to. So the
fee model is not only a cost question, it is a valuation question, and nobody in this market
is writing about it that way.

Second, and just as strong: **an ad account owned by an agency is a diligence problem.** A
buyer is purchasing an acquisition engine. If the account, the pixel, the audiences and the
creative library sit in a provider's Business Manager, the brand does not own the thing the
buyer thinks it is buying, and the learning does not transfer cleanly.

**Both of these are already true on the site**, in /agency-fee, /what-facebook-ads-agencies-charge
and /system. They have simply never been aimed at where the reader is going.

### The destination, in one line

> An account you own, margin that survives your own growth, and a business somebody else could
> run or buy without the whole thing falling over.

Not "cheaper ads". Not "better ROAS".

## What is actually in their way

Researched rather than assumed:

- **Speed.** Creative iteration that takes a boutique five working days runs three weeks
  through a large agency's multi-department process, while the platform rewards fresh creative.
- **They hire before naming the problem.** Most founders sign an agency before defining what
  they need solved, then lose a quarter discovering the team is strong at something else.
- **Brand and performance run as separate workstreams**, producing identity work that does not
  convert and performance work that does not sound like the brand.
- **Optimising toward the wrong signal.** Winning on click-through rate while training the
  platform to find the wrong buyer.
- **The fee compounds with their own growth**, which is the site's existing argument and now
  has somewhere to land.

## How this changes the writing

**Every post starts where they are going, not where the market is broken.** Diagnosis is one
beat inside a post, never the whole of it.

The first-line test: if the subject is Josh, his history, or his opinion, rewrite it so the
subject is the reader and their outcome.

**The arithmetic rule, and it matters.** Do not state what a reader will gain. Give them the
*method* and let them run it on their own numbers, which is exactly what /agency-fee already
does. "Multiply your annual agency fee by the multiple your category trades on, and that is
what the fee costs you at exit" is a framework and is defensible. "You will add $900,000 of
enterprise value" is an invented number and is not.

Market ranges above are quotable **as market research, attributed as such**. They are never
presented as Sevenam's results or as a promise.

## Angles this opens, none of which are being used

- The fee is an EBITDA line item, and EBITDA is what gets multiplied
- What a buyer checks in the ad account during diligence
- Why the median DTC brand misses the margin floor buyers want
- The 18 to 24 month runway, and what to fix in month one rather than month twenty
- Ownership as a transferability question, not a trust question
- Creative velocity as a margin lever rather than a marketing preference
