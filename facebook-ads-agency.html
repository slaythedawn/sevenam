What a Facebook ads agency actually does in 2026.

Most of the work an agency was built around no longer exists. Meta's delivery models decide which ads are eligible for which people and how they're ranked and sequenced. Audience building, manual bid tuning and daily tinkering — the craft the retainer was priced against — stopped driving results, and Meta publishes as much.

What's left is genuinely valuable: deciding what to do about what the platform reports, and producing enough creative for it to have something to test. Those are the only two levers a business still controls. An agency pulls the first lever in a weekly meeting; the system pulls it every morning at seven.

So the question isn't whether you need help with Facebook ads. It's whether you want that help as hours you rent or as a system you own.

Meta automated targeting, ranking and optimisation — that part is not up for debate.
Decision velocity is the first remaining lever: how often somebody looks and acts.
Creative volume is the second: how many distinct ads the engine gets to test.
Everything else on an agency's scope of work is now the platform's job.

What you get instead of a retainer.

The setup takes four weeks. We rebuild the account structure so the delivery system can learn from it, fix tracking and measurement so the numbers you decide on are real, set up a repeatable creative process, and write the decision rules down as a playbook. All of it inside your own Business Manager, so nothing moves if we part ways.

After that, the system runs. Every morning at 7am you get the day's decisions in writing: what to change today, what to leave alone, the numbers behind it and why. About twenty minutes of work a day. No dashboard to log into, no weekly performance call, and no report that needs a human to explain it.

If you also want the creative, we ship a monthly batch of new work with its performance numbers attached, sized to your spend. The setup price is published, and nothing moves because your spend went up.

How to compare us to the agency you're talking to.

Ask three questions. What happens to your fee when your spend doubles? Who owns the account, the pixel and the creative at the end? And what, specifically, does the platform now do that they're still charging for?

Then ask for the fee in writing, as a dollar figure rather than a percentage. Ours is a published one-time setup fee and a fixed monthly, and neither moves when your budget does.

Priced on: a percentage of your media, versus fixed and published.
Cadence: a weekly meeting, versus 07:00, daily, automated.
Ends in: dependency, versus capability you keep.

Questions people actually ask.

Is a Facebook advertising agency different to a Facebook ads agency?

Only in wording — both describe someone running paid campaigns across Facebook and Instagram. What actually differs between suppliers is how the fee is calculated, how often decisions get made, and how much new creative ships each month. Compare those three, not the job title.

Is Sevenam a Facebook ads agency?

Not in the usual sense. We're a growth systems and technology company: we install the system on your own account and run the technology that operates it. We will do the daily execution end to end if you need that, but always for a fixed fee — never a percentage of your media.

Do you charge a percentage of ad spend?

Never. The setup is a fixed one-time price, and the monthly is a flat fee built around your account. What you spend on Meta has no effect on what you pay us.

Who owns the ad account?

You do. Everything is built inside your own Business Manager from hour one — account, pixel, audiences and creative files. If you stop working with us, none of it moves.

What if we have nobody internally to run it?

Then we operate it. Self-run needs one person for about twenty minutes a day; where nobody has that, the end-to-end option covers media buying, creative and the daily execution for a fixed monthly fee, quoted after we read the account.

Do you work with businesses outside Sydney?

Yes. We're based in Sydney and work with businesses across Australia — the system is delivered remotely and the decisions arrive at 7am in your own timezone.
